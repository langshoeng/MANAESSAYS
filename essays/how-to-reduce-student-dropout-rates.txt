How to Reduce Student Dropout Rates

Student dropout is a serious educational and social problem because leaving school early can limit a young person's future opportunities. Students may leave school because of poverty, family responsibilities, poor academic performance, lack of motivation, or difficulties accessing school. In my opinion, dropout rates can be reduced through financial support, stronger relationships with students and families, and better learning experiences.
First, schools and communities can provide support to students who face financial difficulties. Some families may struggle with transportation, school materials, or other costs, while some students may need to work to support their families. For example, scholarships, learning materials, school meal programs, or transportation assistance can help disadvantaged students remain in school.
Second, teachers and parents should identify students at risk of dropping out and provide early support. Students who frequently miss classes, perform poorly, or become disconnected from school may need additional attention. For instance, a teacher can communicate with the student's parents, discuss the reasons for absenteeism, and develop a plan to help the student return to regular attendance.
Third, schools should make learning meaningful and engaging. Students may lose motivation when they do not understand the value of what they are learning. Teachers can use practical activities, projects, career information, and real-life examples to show how education can benefit students' futures. For example, vocational activities can help students understand how school knowledge connects to possible careers.
In conclusion, reducing student dropout requires cooperation among schools, families, communities, and education authorities. Financial support, early intervention, and meaningful learning opportunities can help students remain in school. By addressing the reasons students leave school and providing appropriate support, Cambodia can give more young people the opportunity to complete their education.
